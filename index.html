The homestay

Welcome to Bugun Homestay

A homestay in Bichom, a Bugun village in the hills of West Kameng, about 810 metres up on the Bichom river.

Rooms are simple and the food is what the village eats. The days are yours: the river and the bridge, the path up to Lichini, the bamboo and the nettle fibre, and the forest of the birds higher up the hill.

Things to do

Bridge, river, forest, and the hill above

Nothing is scheduled. Tell us what you'd like to do, and we'll take it from there.

The wooden bridge
The bridge

Cross the old wooden bridge

A suspension footbridge of cable, logs and planks, with the river running fast underneath. It's the way across, and the place everyone stops for a photograph. Ask us its story.

The river
The river

A morning by the river

The Bichom has two Bugun names: Diga Khow, the male, and Khatong Khow, the female. Khow is water. It runs past the village to meet the Kameng, where oral history places the hunter Bishum Shapa.

Fishing
Fishing

Fish with a net, or with a rod

Come out with us and try both: casting a net, and a rod from the bank. Nets here were once made from Shaung, a nettle-like plant from the hill. You'll need a permit from the district fisheries office, so tell us when you book. Golden mahseer always go back in.

The Lichini hike
The hike

Walk up to Lichini

Lichini is one of the older Bugun villages, and families came down from it to settle Bichom. From the road end above the village it is about 6.5 km on foot, climbing some 800 m to Lichini at around 1,680 m. A recorded walk took three and a half hours, with stops. On the way you pass three wild fibre plants, one after another as the ground rises.

Bamboo
Bamboo

Bamboo, everywhere you look

Carrying baskets, fish carriers, rice cleaners, strainers, bags: in a Bugun house much of daily life is woven from bamboo. Two bamboo craftsmen work at the top of the village road, and even the spindle for nettle thread is a bamboo shaft with a hook cut into it.

Food
Food

Eat what the village eats

Pork is the centre of a Bugun feast, and meat is smoked on the rack above the hearth. Some food is still cooked the old way: packed into green bamboo and stood in the fire. Tell us before you come if there's anything you can't eat.

About the birds

Nettle fibre

From the plant on the hill to the thread

The Bugun once made thread from the bark of nettles and their relatives. The cloth stopped generations ago, when ready-made thread came up the hill from Assam. In Bichom two men have relearned the whole process from memory, and in 2026 it was recorded from the standing plant to the finished thread.

HanchawHimalayan giant nettle. It stings, and spins a pale cream thread.
ShaungA false-nettle that doesn't sting. Bowstrings and fishing nets were made of it. Spins brown.
ShaungmauhRamie, planted beside the house rather than gathered.
Shaung BamungThe smallest of the Shaung group, also used for nets.
MikhileeAn elm, newly tried. The whitest fibre of them all.
MikhisheatThe second new one, found growing beside Mikhilee.
  1. Cut and peelJune to August, green stems only. Peeled and boiled the same day, or the bark goes hard.
  2. Boil with oak ashAn iron pan, sieved oak ash, seven to eight hours over a steady fire.
  3. Beat, rinse, dryStruck on a rock at the river and rinsed until it washes white, then hung in the sun.
  4. ClayA soft pale clay, dug and sieved, is worked in. Five or six days drying, then it is beaten out.
  5. CardDrawn between two toothed cards until the fibre opens.
  6. SpinOn the Chhauk, a bamboo spindle with a hard seed for a whorl.

If the fibre is being worked while you're here, ask whether you can watch. The knowledge belongs to the people of Bichom and Lichini who shared it, and where the wild plants grow is not shared.

From Revival of Memory, a field record of Bugun fibre work at Bichom, September–October 2026. Plates and photographs by the field author.

Birds

A bird that lives only on Bugun land

In 1995 a bird was found in the forest above Singchung that lives nowhere else on earth. In 2006 Ramana Athreya described it and named it for the people whose land it lives on: the Bugun Liocichla.

It is critically endangered. By one count only 14 to 20 birds hold a few square kilometres of temperate forest, between about 1,850 and 2,450 metres, within the traditional lands of Singchung village. In 2017 the Bugun protected 17 km² of that forest as the Singchung Bugun Village Community Reserve, the first community reserve in Arunachal Pradesh.

Bichom is at about 810 m, well below the liocichla's forest. Seeing it means a day trip up to Lama Camp and the reserve, around 2,300 m. April and May, when pairs are singing, give the best chance. Ask us when you book.

History and people

The Bugun, and how Bichom began

The Bugun are a small community: a village-by-village count in 2022 put them at 2,075 people in twelve villages, all in the Singchung sub-division. Bichom, with 377, is the second largest.

Their language belongs to the small Kho-Bwa group, spoken only in this corner of the eastern Himalaya, and has no script: what is remembered is remembered by being said. The group's name was made from these languages' words for fire and water, and in Bichom khow is still water, in the river's two names, and in Pham Kho, where pham is the mountain.

The clans at Bichom, Lichini, Ramu and Chitu are Lali, Bachung and Hagam, and trace their descent to a single forefather, Achinphumphulua. Farming is the base of the year, with fishing beside it, and the harvest festival, Pham Kho Sowai, falls on 10 September. The older belief is animist; Mahayana Buddhism reached these valleys from the Sherdukpen in the early twentieth century.

Bichom itself is young: a model village settled by families from Lichini, Ramu, Chitu and Sachida. Older than the village is the story of the Bishum Shapa, the hunter who came from where the Bichom meets the Kameng.

Getting here

Up from the Assam plains, through Bhalukpong

  1. Fly to Tezpur in Assam, or to Guwahati, which has more flights. The nearest railway station is Bhalukpong.
  2. Drive up through Bhalukpong and Tenga, on the road into West Kameng.
  3. On to Bichom, past Singchung and the Tenga market, which lie about 13 and 17 km to the south-west as the crow flies. We send exact directions when you book.

Questions

Before you come

Do I need a permit to visit?

Yes. Indian visitors from outside Arunachal need an Inner Line Permit (e-ILP), applied for online on the official portal. Foreign visitors need a Protected Area Permit through the e-FRRO portal, officially for groups of two or more, applied for at least 30 days ahead. Children under 14 with a permit-holding adult don't need their own e-ILP.

How do we get there?

Fly to Tezpur or Guwahati in Assam, or take the train to Bhalukpong, then drive up through Bhalukpong and Tenga into West Kameng. Tell us where you're starting from and we'll send directions and help you plan the drive.

When is the best time to come?

November to May. March to May is the best time for birds; October to April for fishing. The monsoon, June to September, brings heavy rain and landslides on the roads.

Will we see the Bugun Liocichla?

Maybe. It's very rare, and lives in the forest between about 1,850 and 2,450 m, a day trip up from the village to Lama Camp and the community reserve. April and May, when pairs are singing, give the best chance. Go with a local guide and stay on the paths.

Can we go fishing?

Yes, with a net or a rod, but you'll need a permit from the district fisheries office, and it isn't usually issued in the monsoon. Golden mahseer are always released. Tell us when you book so we can plan for it.

Can we see the nettle fibre being made?

Sometimes. The bark is gathered from June to August and the work is done by hand, a stage at a time, when there is time for it. If it's being worked while you're here, ask whether you can watch.

What should we pack?

Good walking shoes, a rain jacket in any season, and warm layers for the day trip up to the birding forest, which is around 2,300 m and much colder than the village.
